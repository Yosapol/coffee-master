Coffee troubleshooting

Fix the cup by changing one thing.

Pick what feels off. Change one thing. Taste again.

LET YOUR CUP DO THE TALKING

What would you like to change?

Bitter or drying?

Bitter is a taste; drying is a feeling left in your mouth. They can occur together. Choose the closest problem below and treat the adjustment as a starting point.

Change order

Adjust one variable first.

  1. Grind sizeMost powerful flavor control for most brews.
  2. RatioChange strength without confusing extraction too much.
  3. Water temperatureUse hotter for light roasts, cooler for dark roasts.
  4. Time or pouringFine-tune contact time after grind and ratio feel close.

Brew log

Remember your last good cup.

Track coffee dose, water amount, grind setting, water temperature, brew time, and taste. Change only one line on the next brew.

Keep the last setting that worked. If an adjustment makes the cup worse, return to it before trying another variable.

YOUR NEXT LITTLE STEP

Give your adjustment a fair try.

Repeat the same recipe and compare the taste. If your beans are past their best, explore a fresh starting point.
