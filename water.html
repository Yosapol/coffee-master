Cleanliness

Start with filtered drinking water.

Chlorine, odor, and old filters can flatten good coffee. If tap water smells like chlorine, tastes salty, or leaves heavy scale, filter it or use bottled water with a clean taste.

Hardness & alkalinity

Control hardness and alkalinity.

Calcium and magnesium contribute to hardness and affect extraction and body. Too little mineral can taste thin; too much hardness can mute clarity. Alkalinity buffers acidity, and too much can make it dull.

Fine-tuning

Season water for the brew goal.

Use magnesium-forward water for brighter filter coffee, balanced calcium-magnesium water for body, and controlled alkalinity for acidity.

Troubleshooting

Water symptoms and practical fixes.

Flat and dull
Possible cause
High alkalinity or old coffee
First adjustment
Try lower alkalinity water and compare with the same recipe.
Sharp but weak
Possible cause
Very low minerals or under-extraction
First adjustment
Use balanced mineral water, then grind slightly finer if needed.
Dry and harsh
Possible cause
High hardness, high extraction, or too hot
First adjustment
Reduce extraction first, then test softer brew water.
Scale in kettle
Possible cause
Hard water
First adjustment
Use filtration or blend hard water with purified water.
